---
name: Pre-call brief
description: Use when I'm about to get on a sales or discovery call and give you a company name, website or a person's name and role. Produces a one-screen brief I can read in two minutes.
---

# Goal
A short brief that helps me open the call with a real reason, ask better questions, and avoid saying anything that isn't true.

# What I'll give you
- The company name or website (required)
- The person's name and role (optional)
- What I sell, in one line (optional, but makes the brief much better)

# Steps
1. Find what the company sells and who it sells to, in one plain sentence.
2. Look for one recent reason to reach out from the last 90 days: hiring for a role, funding, a launch, a new leader, an expansion, or news. Note the date and the link.
3. If I gave a person, find what they own in their role and anything they've posted or said publicly recently.
4. Connect the dots: based on step 2, what problem might they be dealing with right now that relates to what I sell? Mark this as a guess.
5. Write 3 discovery questions that test that guess without leading them.
6. Write one opening line that mentions the reason from step 2 in a natural way.

# Rules
- Every fact about the company or person gets a source link next to it.
- If you can't find something, write "not found". Never fill a gap with a guess dressed up as a fact.
- Step 4 is always labelled "My guess" so I know it isn't confirmed.
- Plain words, no buzzwords, no flattery in the opener.
- Whole brief under 200 words.

# Output format
**Company:** one sentence (source)
**Why now:** the recent reason, with date (source), or "not found"
**The person:** role and one recent detail (source), or "not found"
**My guess at the problem:** one or two sentences
**Questions to ask:**
1.
2.
3.
**Opener:** one sentence

# Example of a good result
**Company:** Acme Analytics sells reporting software to mid-size retail and SaaS teams (acme.example/about)
**Why now:** posted 3 SDR roles on Oct 2 (acme.example/careers)
**The person:** Head of Sales, posted last week about ramp time for new reps (linkedin link)
**My guess at the problem:** with 3 new SDRs starting, they'll need them booking meetings fast, and ramp time is already on their mind.
**Questions to ask:**
1. How long does it take a new SDR to book their first meeting today?
2. What do new reps get on day one to learn who to call and why?
3. Where do they get stuck most in the first month?
**Opener:** "Saw you're bringing on three SDRs, and your post on ramp time made me curious how you're planning their first month."

(Example data: Acme Analytics is made up, to show the format.)
