# Hiring Manager's Vetting Checklist

For evaluating a GTM engineer candidate's claimed revenue impact — real questions, not gut feel.

## Before You Even Get To Revenue Numbers

**Ask:** "Tell me about a time you were asked to automate something, and you pushed back or changed the approach first."

The most common GTM engineering failure isn't a bad build — it's automating a process that was already broken (wrong ICP, bad scoring logic, irrelevant triggers) and making the mistake happen faster. A candidate who's never questioned a process before automating it is a red flag, regardless of how technical they are.

**Look for:** a specific process they changed or refused to automate as-is.
**Red flag:** answers that only describe tools used, never a judgment call made.

## When They Give You A Revenue Number

Any claim like "I drove $2M in pipeline" or "cut CAC by 30%" needs three follow-ups before you take it at face value:

**1. "What was the baseline before this shipped, and over what time period?"**
No baseline means no real comparison. A number with no "before" is a guess wearing a decimal point.

**2. "What else changed at the same time that could explain the result?"**
If a new SDR team also started that quarter, or pricing changed, the automation might get credit for someone else's work.

**3. "Who else can independently confirm this number?"**
A real result usually has a manager, a dashboard, or a teammate who'd say the same thing. A number that only the candidate can vouch for is worth less than one that's cross-checked.

**What a real answer sounds like:** immediate, specific, with a number that moves slightly when pressed (because it's remembered, not rehearsed).

**What a weak answer sounds like:** a pause, a restatement of the same resume line, or a number that gets rounder when you ask for detail instead of more specific.

## Setting Expectations After You Hire

GTM engineering ROI compounds — it doesn't arrive on day one. Set three checkpoints before their start date, and share them on day one:

- **Day 30:** one small, working automation. Not transformational — working.
- **Day 90:** measurable time or cost savings on at least one workflow.
- **Month 6:** a compounding system — several automations working together, not a single script. Real published examples show this stage replacing the equivalent of 3-4 additional manual hires.

Most of the friction in this role comes from measuring someone against month-6 expectations on day 14.
