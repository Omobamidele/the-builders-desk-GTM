# How to write cold emails (Builder's Desk Week 12)

**For:** SDRs, founders, and GTM builders who keep rewriting copy when replies are low.
**What you get:** a one-page formula card + a Claude skill that writes a 3–4 step sequence without inventing facts.

## The idea (plain English)

A cold email is the first note to someone who doesn't know you. It's not a pitch deck in an inbox.

Good ones work like a note to a neighbour: short, about them, one easy question.

Most people pay freelancers $1k–2.5k to set up Instantly sequences. The writing rules behind those installs are simple — and most teams break them before they hit send.

## The formula (memorise this)

1. **One persona, one problem, one email**
2. **Lead with a real reason** (a signal you can point to: hiring, tech install, news, job post) — or say you don't have one and keep it short
3. **Under ~100 words** on email 1
4. **One small ask** (a yes/no or a short note), not a demo CTA
5. **Email 2 gives something useful** before asking again
6. **Never** fake Re:, fake "quick question", invented results, or "I noticed your company is growing"

## How to use the skill

1. Drop `cold-email-writer` into your Claude skills folder (or paste SKILL.md into a Project)
2. Tell Claude: write a cold email sequence for [persona] about [problem]
3. Give it a signal if you have one, or say "no signal"
4. It must ask for anything missing before writing
5. Every real-company fact needs a source; gaps say "not found"

## Honest limits

- This kit does not fix deliverability, list quality, or a bad offer. Check those before you rewrite copy again.
- No reply-rate promises. Numbers depend on your list, domain, and offer.
- Example company names are fictional.
