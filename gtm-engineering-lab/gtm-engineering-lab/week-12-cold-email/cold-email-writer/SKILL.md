# Sequence writer

**Level:** Beginner · **Area:** Copy, sequences and multichannel

## Explain it like I'm 10
A good cold email is like a note to a neighbour: short, about them, with one easy question. Not a flyer pushed through every door.

## What I need from you
- gtm-context.md
- Persona
- Signal (from signal-finder) if any

Ask for anything missing, then wait for the answers before starting.

## Steps
1. Pick one problem for one persona. One email, one idea.
2. Email 1: the reason you're writing (signal), the problem in their words, one small ask. Aim for under about 100 words.
3. Email 2: give something useful (an idea, a short checklist, a relevant example), no new ask or the same small ask.
4. Email 3: a different angle on the problem.
5. Email 4: a polite close-the-loop.
6. Write 2 subject line options per email. Plain, lowercase is fine, no tricks.
7. Make one A/B variant of email 1 to test a single change.
8. Run compliance-check before sending.

## Rules
- No fake familiarity ("Re:" on a first email, fake "quick question").
- Never claim results the user hasn't confirmed.
- Every fact about a real company or person gets a source link. If you can't find it, write "not found".
- Label guesses as "My guess". Never dress a guess up as a fact.
- Never invent numbers, results or customer names.
- Plain words. If a term is needed, explain it in one short sentence.
- Read `gtm-context.md` first if it exists.

## Output
A 3 to 4 step sequence with subject lines, send-day gaps and one A/B variant.

## Example
_Example only: "Acme Analytics" is a made-up company used to show the format._

```
Example (made up, for Acme Analytics):
Subject: ad spend question
Hi Sam, saw you're hiring a paid ads lead. When a new hire joins, the first question is usually which ads actually make money. Worth a short note on how other shops check that?
```
