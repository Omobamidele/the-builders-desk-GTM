Tasks In GTM Engineering That AI Cannot Do For You

This is the gift for week two. No paywall, no email required, nobody selling you a course after this. Just open it and use it.

Most of what people hand to AI in this job, AI should have. Cleaning lists, drafting sequences, writing the script, summarizing the call, building the dashboard query. Give all of that away.

But there is a specific category of work that keeps getting handed over anyway, and it should not be. Not because AI is weak at it, but because AI does not have the thing the task actually runs on: your real business data, and your context around it.

Here is that category, task by task.

1. Defining your ICP

Ask AI who your ideal customer is and it will describe the market. It will give you a company size, an industry, a title, and it will sound reasonable.

It has no access to your closed won data. It cannot see that your best accounts by retention are the ones your sales team complains are too small, or that the segment everyone is chasing churns at twice the rate. Only your own numbers tell you that, and you have to go pull them.

The task: open your CRM. Look at your last fifty closed won and your last fifty churned. Find what they actually have in common. That is your ICP. Everything else is a guess wearing a suit.

2. Making the call on pricing and discounting

AI can produce a pricing framework. It cannot tell you what your margin actually is on this deal, what you promised this customer last quarter, what precedent this discount sets with the two other accounts in the same industry who talk to each other, or whether your CFO is currently under pressure on realized price.

The task: know your numbers before you decide, not after. If you cannot say what this decision costs the business in real terms, you are not ready to make it.

3. Deciding what to automate

This is the one people get backwards most often. AI will happily help you build the thing. It will never tell you the thing should not exist.

It does not know that the process you are automating only runs eleven times a quarter, or that the team is about to be restructured, or that the manual version works fine and the real bottleneck is two steps upstream.

The task: before you build, count. How often does this run, how long does it take, what does it actually cost you today. If you cannot show the math, you are automating for the feeling of it.

4. Choosing tools

Every tool comparison AI gives you is assembled from marketing pages and reviews. None of it accounts for what you already own, what your team will actually adopt, what your data looks like going in, or the fact that half your problem is that you already bought three things nobody uses.

The task: audit what you have first. Most tool decisions are really adoption problems, and no comparison table will surface that.

5. Reading whether the system is working

A dashboard reports what it was told to report. When something breaks upstream, the number does not go red. It stays confident and becomes wrong, and it will stay wrong until a person goes and looks at the raw data.

The task: sample the raw records yourself on a schedule. Not to be thorough. Because that is the only way this class of failure gets caught at all.

6. Owning the decision afterward

You can automate the analysis. You cannot automate accountability. When leadership asks why a number moved, why a segment was deprioritized, why a deal got approved at that price, there is no version of this job where the tool answers.

The task: be able to say why, in your own words, without naming a tool. If your only answer is a citation, you did not make the decision. You forwarded it.

The pattern

Every task on this list fails the same way. AI can reason. It cannot know your business. The moment a task depends on your actual numbers, your actual history with a customer, your actual team, or your actual constraints, the work stops being generatable and starts being yours.

Automate the execution. Keep the judgment. That line is where your job lives, and it is the part that gets more valuable as everything around it gets automated.
